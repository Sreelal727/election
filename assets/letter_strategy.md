# LETTER CAMPAIGN STRATEGY

## Which Constituencies To Send Letters To

Don't send to all 140. Send to 8-12 carefully selected seats where:
- You have strong data confidence
- The seat is competitive (toss-up or lean)
- UDF campaign leadership is known/accessible for delivery

### Recommended Targets (adjust based on your data confidence):

TIER 1 — Must Send (you have deep data):
  1. Kochi (80) — Full booth-level analysis available
  2. Kalamassery (77) — Ward-level analysis available

TIER 2 — Send If Predictions Are Sharp:
  3-4 toss-up seats where your model shows a close margin
  5-6 seats where UDF is projected to flip from 2021

TIER 3 — Strategic Sends:
  7-8 high-profile constituencies (CM's seat, opposition leader's seat, etc.)

## Letter Variations By Data Depth

### FULL VERSION (Kochi/Kalamassery — where you have booth data):
- Include specific booth numbers, vote counts, zone analysis
- Include community-level breakdown
- 2-3 pages, highly detailed

### LITE VERSION (other constituencies — Monte Carlo only):
- Overall margin prediction with confidence range
- Top-level swing direction (LSG-based)
- 3-5 key vulnerabilities (generic but constituency-specific)
- 1 page maximum

Example lite version opening:

    "UDF is projected to win [constituency] by [X,000 - Y,000] votes.
     However, LSG 2025 data shows a [Z%] swing that, if not sustained,
     narrows this to [A,000 - B,000]. Key risk: [one-line risk]."

## Timing

- Send letters April 1-2 (gives campaign 5-6 days to act)
- Do NOT send on April 3+ (too late to act = letter feels useless)
- Do NOT send after election (feels like showing off, not helping)

## Delivery Method

Option A (Best): Through your UDF contact — he hands it to campaign leaders
  Pro: Trusted channel, guaranteed delivery
  Con: Your contact becomes identifiable

Option B (Most Mysterious): Courier service, no sender details
  Pro: Maximum mystery
  Con: Might get ignored as spam/threat

Option C (Balanced): Your contact delivers sealed envelope, says
  "This came from a data team I know. Just read it."
  Pro: Trust + mystery balanced
  Con: Contact needs to be willing

RECOMMENDATION: Option C. Your contact is the trusted bridge.
He doesn't need to explain who you are. Just: "read it."

## The Follow-Up Play (Post-Election)

After results on April 8:

1. Send a SECOND letter to the same campaign leaders:

    "Ref: GC/KOC/2026/0401 (our pre-election brief dated 01 April 2026)

     Predicted margin: 12,000 - 16,000
     Actual margin: [actual]

     Predicted NDA booths won: 3 (Chellanam zone)
     Actual: [actual]

     Our pre-election brief identified [X] vulnerabilities.
     [Y] of these materialized as predicted.

     If your leadership would like a comprehensive 140-constituency
     post-election analysis, we are open to a conversation.

                                                    — GreyCell
                                                    [email address — NOW you include it]"

   THIS is the letter that gets you the meeting. The first letter
   was the setup. The second letter is the proof.

2. The gap between "no contact info" (letter 1) and "here's how
   to reach us" (letter 2) is intentional:
   - Letter 1: "We don't need you"
   - Letter 2: "But we're open to working together"
   - This is classic negotiation positioning

## What NOT To Do

- Do NOT send to both UDF and LDF in the same constituency
  (pick one side — you're positioning for KPCC/INC)
- Do NOT send to NDA/BJP (you're building a relationship with INC)
- Do NOT include anything that could be read as a threat
- Do NOT claim to have "inside information" — say "electoral data analysis"
- Do NOT mention specific candidates by name — keep it about votes and booths
- Do NOT exaggerate your predictions — if you're unsure, widen the range
